# Creative quality gates

## Gate 1: Art direction
- Is the concept specific to the brand and explainable beyond 'premium', 'futuristic' or 'immersive'?
- Do hierarchy, composition, imagery, type, materials and empty space have a coherent rationale?
- Is there a signature moment that is earned by the brand story rather than borrowed from an award-site reference?
- Are obvious AI/scaffold clichés present without justification?
- Does every section establish a different informational purpose while belonging to one system?

## Gate 2: 3D asset & scene
- Silhouette/proportions, topology, UV, texture/material realism, lighting, contact cues, camera discipline, motion, browser optimization and consistency across views.
- Can it be shown in a neutral studio turntable without its visual quality collapsing?
- Is it a finished asset, or only a proxy? Label appropriately.

## Gate 3: UX / production
- Real user tasks, content accuracy, primary action, accessibility, input methods, responsiveness, loading, fallback and reduced motion.
- Test mobile and desktop actual rendered screenshots and functional interactions, not code alone, if supported.

## Critical review method
- Record 5 biggest specific defects, tied to exact location/asset; rank by severity.
- Fix the first 3, regenerate/inspect, repeat until no blocker remains or a limit is reached.
- If there is no tool to view output, disclose 'not visually verified' and request review instead of passing the gate.
- Distinguish human subjectivity from objective errors. No invented originality percentage or fake design score.

## Mini evaluation set
1. Cosmetic bottle: not just a default floating glossy bottle on gradient; develop visual story using actual product specifics and packaging constraints.
2. Property showcase: avoid generic luxury hero cards; devise grounded spatial exploration and a simple fallback path to floor plans and contact.
3. Creative agency: avoid dark background + floating chrome blob; use evidence of projects and a narrative of its work.
4. Signed sneakers: maintain exact scan fidelity, meaningful signature inspection, smooth touch/desktop interaction and clear inquiry path.

Compare the candidate against an intentionally generic version. Specify what changed in composition, storytelling, modeling and interaction, and whether that change helps the user.
