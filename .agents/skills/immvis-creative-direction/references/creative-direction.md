# Creative-direction protocol

## Input brief (obtain or state assumptions)
- Brand/product truth, intended audience, context of use, desired business conversion.
- Three adjectives desired and three prohibited; evidence such as packaging, product design, photography or architecture.
- Existing brand system, available 3D/CAD/photos/videos, user devices, schedule and delivery scope.

## Three territories MUST vary structurally
For each describe:
1. Core metaphor (specific to client).
2. Visual composition with actual spatial relations and scale, not generic adjectives.
3. Type system: proportions, typographic contrast and information density (not unlicensed font dependencies).
4. Palette and material language tied to the metaphor.
5. Interface behavior and interaction affordances.
6. Hero choreography and transition beats.
7. The 3D object(s) that embody the product and why.
8. Originality risk, feasibility, mobile alternative.

## Distinctiveness stress test
- Remove the logo: could this page still be connected to the client? If not, rework it.
- Replace the company name with any generic startup: does the page still work unchanged? If yes, rework the concept.
- Can the hero be explained as 'gradient + heading + 3D thing'? If yes, revise.
- Is the visual hierarchy carried by layout, copy, typography, image and spatial composition — rather than by novelty effects alone?
- Identify at least three decisions not derivable from a common SaaS landing-page scaffold.

## Human presentation
Give concept boards/storyboard frames when tool access allows. Label generated or provisional assets as such. Approval should specify chosen concept, rejected details, and constraints for production.
